Shareholders file a proposed class action against chipmaker Nvidia and its chief executive after reports detail an affair with a suspected intelligence asset.

Nvidia and Chief Executive Jensen Huang were sued in a proposed class action filed in federal court in San Jose, California, on Monday, November 10, 2026. The lawsuit accuses the defendants of concealing his relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, raising questions about security at the world's most valuable artificial intelligence chipmaker. The legal action centers on Jensen Huang China spy ties and whether national security concerns and risks to business prospects were properly disclosed to investors.

Key Facts

  • The Lawsuit: Filed by Nvidia shareholders in federal court in San Jose, California, on November 10, 2026.
  • The Allegations: Nvidia and Jensen Huang allegedly misled investors by failing to disclose national security risks tied to an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed.
  • Company Response: Nvidia announced an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.
  • Market Impact: Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% on Monday, while the lawsuit was filed five minutes after US markets closed.

Shareholders Sue Nvidia and Jensen Huang Over Alleged Intelligence Asset Ties

The proposed class action adds to ongoing investigations into whether Jensen Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence. Shareholders assert that Nvidia and Huang misled investors regarding his relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based cloud provider Megaspeed and a top Nvidia customer, who is not related to the chief executive. According to The New York Times, US authorities concluded that Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset who funneled information straight to the Chinese government.

Nvidia announced an independent investigation on Sunday, November 9, 2026, into reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Jensen Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife in an open letter, though he did not identify the woman. "I support the investigation and will cooperate fully," he stated, adding that he is "committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America's national security." Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% on Monday, with the lawsuit filed five minutes after US markets closed.

Washington and Beijing Respond to Intelligence Allegations

US President Donald Trump stated on Monday that the FBI was investigating the matter while expressing support for Huang. Trump told reporters that he had spoken to the executive earlier that day, noting that Huang said he had absolutely no idea that the person in question was a spy. Meanwhile, China denied conducting an intelligence operation targeting Huang. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun stated that the so-called Chinese spy allegation is unsubstantiated and constitutes smears against China.

US officials believe Megaspeed is connected to a convoluted web of Chinese companies working together to access chips in bulk and potentially get restricted chips into China, with Alibaba reportedly listed as one of Megaspeed's top customers. Trade records from ImportGenius cited by The New York Times indicate that Megaspeed's sister company, headquartered in Malaysia, spent $4.8 billion or more on Nvidia chips and technology between June 2024 and February 2026.

Export Controls and Lobbying Efforts Under US Scrutiny

The controversy surrounds Jensen Huang's unique position to influence AI export controls. Hanna Dohmen, a senior research analyst at CSET, noted in an interview with The Verge that an alleged spy would potentially be interested in understanding US export control policies, gathering information on Huang's conversations with the Trump administration, attempting to influence policy, and collecting trade secrets.

Nvidia spokespeople previously described Huang's relationship with the Megaspeed executive as strictly business, stating that meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job. However, US officials reportedly surveilled the Megaspeed executive for months and found she was in close contact with Huang. Culper Research noted that a dinner in Taipei occurred in May 2025 shortly after Nvidia shared new restrictions on its China-specific H20 chips. Months later, Huang met with Trump in the Oval Office in July 2025 to plead his case for loosening export restrictions on advanced semiconductors, and the company announced days later that the administration had blessed sales of H20 chips to China.

Market Reaction and the Economic Footprint of Nvidia

Nvidia controls an estimated 90% of the market for computer chips used in major AI projects driving the US economy, boasting a market capitalization of about $4.25 trillion, second only to Apple. CSET's Dohmen pointed out that even without Chinese intelligence operations, it is in Nvidia's interest and Jensen Huang's interest to lobby for looser export control restrictions, as any restrictions stifle business expansion and cut off potential sales.

President Trump praised Huang during a speech last year, noting that after meeting the executive, he understood the difficulty of re-creating the company Huang built. Following lobbying efforts, Trump changed course in December 2025 to allow Nvidia to sell H20 chips to an approved list of customers in China, with the US government taking a 25 percent cut of those sales.

Next Steps in the Nvidia Independent Investigation

Nvidia's board of directors has opened an official investigation into Jensen Huang following his admission of the affair. Federal authorities, including the FBI as confirmed by President Trump, are continuing their investigations into the matter alongside the ongoing shareholder class action in the federal court in San Jose, California.

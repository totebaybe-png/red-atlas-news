Nvidia CEO Affair China Spy Fallout: Jensen Huang Probed

By Red Atlas News Desk

File photo: NVIDIA headquarters at 2788-2888 San Tomas Expressway in Santa Clara, California.. Photo: Coolcaesar / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

Jensen Huang faces FBI scrutiny, an internal board inquiry, and congressional calls to resign following his relationship with an alleged Beijing operative.

WASHINGTON — Federal scrutiny deepened on Wednesday over the Nvidia ceo affair China spy controversy as United States intelligence agencies and lawmakers assessed whether national security or advanced semiconductor secrets were compromised. Jensen Huang, the co-founder and chief executive of Nvidia, has admitted to an extramarital relationship with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, whom American intelligence officials suspect of operating on behalf of Beijing's Ministry of State Security.

Key facts on the investigation

  • CEO Admission: Jensen Huang admitted to being unfaithful to his wife and issued an apology to his family, while Nvidia's board launched an independent investigation.
  • Espionage Allegations: US intelligence officials concluded that Alice Huang, who is of no relation to Jensen Huang, was recruited by China to help secure access to advanced semiconductors.
  • Corporate and Federal Probes: The Federal Bureau of Investigation, the US Department of Justice, and Singapore authorities are conducting inquiries connected to the matter and linked companies.
  • Resignation Demands: Republican Senator Kevin Cramer called on Jensen Huang to step down, citing the danger of foreign extortion.
  • Market Control: Nvidia controls more than 80 percent of the market for high-end artificial intelligence graphics processing units.

Background of the Nvidia CEO affair China spy allegations

The controversy emerged following reports by The New York Times revealing that US intelligence officials tracked communications between Alice Huang and an American citizen later identified as Jensen Huang. Intelligence officials believe Alice Huang was tasked by Beijing's Ministry of State Security with influencing Nvidia to expand the availability of its high-end processors in China.

Alice Huang, who also uses the name Huang Le, built her early career in Chinese finance. Records from the Chinese private equity database Simuwang and corporate filings show she previously worked at CITIC Securities, the Hong Kong private banking branch of HSBC, and Shanghai Hexi Investment Co. between 2015 and 2017. Chinese regulators fined Hexi 17.7 million yuan ($2.7 million) and its founder Kang Wei 400,000 yuan ($60,000) for financial misconduct in 2018.

In March 2023, Alice Huang became a director and controlling shareholder at Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based data center company spun out from Chinese video game developer 7Road Holdings Ltd. Megaspeed operates as an Nvidia cloud partner. Public filings indicate that she transferred her shares to Singaporean businessman Tan Yong Pong, also known as James Tan, in November 2023. A May report by short-seller Culper Research cited an employee who stated Alice Huang remained involved in daily operations as the firm's boss. Her current location is unknown, and she has not responded to requests for comment.

A photograph posted to LinkedIn in 2024 by software firm NetThunder showed Alice Huang seated behind Jensen Huang during an event in Taipei. China's Foreign Ministry rejected the espionage claims. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun stated: “The allegations about Chinese spies are entirely groundless, speculative and intended to smear and malign China.”

Federal investigations and Singapore export scrutiny

President Donald Trump stated on Monday that the FBI is examining the matter. In addition, the US Department of Justice opened an investigation into Megaspeed regarding whether the company diverted restricted high-end processors to Chinese buyers. Trade records indicate that more than $3 billion in semiconductors moved from Singapore to China over a single year, accompanied by substantial shipments routed through Malaysia.

Singapore's government announced that it is investigating Megaspeed, stating it does not condone efforts to circumvent foreign export controls. Singapore authorities confirmed they are not conducting a separate investigation specifically into Alice Huang. Megaspeed previously denied illicit activity, publishing a statement on its website asserting that “there has been no evidence of any violations concerning the illicit transfer of chips or any illegal activities.”

Nvidia retained New York law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to carry out an independent inquiry on behalf of its board. The review is led by Joon Kim, the former US Attorney for the Southern District of New York. An Nvidia spokesperson told the BBC: “We trust that if we were the target of a genuine threat, the US government would have notified us.” The company stated it does not believe corporate data or national security suffered any breach.

Congressional backlash and resignation calls

Lawmakers on Capitol Hill have voiced alarm over the potential exposure of proprietary semiconductor data. Former Pentagon intelligence official Retired General Leslie Beavers told Politico that authorities must determine whether Chinese entities obtained insights into chip manufacturing processes, performance, and reliability. Glenn Gerstell, former general counsel for the National Security Agency, told Politico that the private sector was “every bit as important to our national security as the government is.”

Republican Senator Kevin Cramer of the Senate Banking Committee demanded that Jensen Huang resign from his post at Nvidia. “The potential for extortion is so tremendous that something as sensitive as Nvidia chips, I just think he's got to go,” Cramer told Politico. “If the worst thing he did in real life is infidelity with this woman who may or may not be a Chinese spy — but she might be — that's enough, because whatever else there is, we don't know. And to me, that's a security risk.”

Other lawmakers have demanded formal hearings. Representative Ro Khanna, the ranking Democrat on the House Select Committee on China, called for classified briefings on the potential security exposure. The leaders of the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and China committees have demanded a joint briefing from the executive.

AI dominance, export controls, and Trump administration ties

Nvidia holds an estimated share of more than 80 percent of the global market for high-end AI processors, which were initially engineered for computer graphics before becoming fundamental for training large AI models. The US government has imposed strict export restrictions since 2022 to limit Chinese access to advanced computing chips, which officials say could bolster Beijing's military and surveillance systems. Due to these rules, Jensen Huang previously stated that Nvidia's share of the Chinese chip market dropped from approximately 95 percent to effectively zero.

Jensen Huang had spent months conducting shuttle diplomacy between Washington and Beijing, arguing that the US should permit the sale of less advanced processors, such as H200 chips, to keep Chinese firms tied to the American computing ecosystem. Last spring, Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the drafting of an internal memo alerting the Trump administration to Huang's contacts with Alice Huang. Rubio declined to comment when questioned about the matter.

Trump was briefed on intelligence regarding Alice Huang last year. When asked on Sunday if he should have intervened sooner, Trump told reporters: “No, I don't think so. I think he's [Jensen Huang] an honourable guy.” Trump said he discussed the matter directly with Huang on Monday. “Did you have any idea she was a spy?” Trump said he asked. “He said 'I had no idea she was a spy' and I really believe him.” Trump added: “I think he’ll be in good shape.”

Huang, who has an estimated fortune of $155 billion according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index, has been a prominent industry partner of the White House. He appeared beside Trump and Elon Musk at a White House AI summit attended by 42 technology executives, and he accompanied Trump on Air Force One during a state visit to Beijing earlier this year.

What happens next

House Foreign Affairs Committee leaders have summoned Jensen Huang to appear for a joint, closed-door briefing on November 18, 2026. The session is scheduled to cover his interactions with Alice Huang and Nvidia's internal security countermeasures against foreign intelligence gathering.

A person familiar with the committee's request told the BBC that Jensen Huang has not yet stated whether he will attend. In parallel, Wachtell Lipton's independent investigation under Joon Kim continues to review executive communications, while the FBI and the Department of Justice proceed with their inquiries into Megaspeed's chip supply channels.

Infrastructure Crippled by Russian Ballistic Missiles and Drones

Ukrainian authorities reported that Saturday's overnight strikes caused severe disruption across Kyiv and 12 other regions. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed that Russian forces launched 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones across the country. Zelenskyy noted that Ukrainian air defense forces were able to intercept only four of the ballistic missiles due to a critical shortage of Patriot interceptor missiles, adding that preliminary findings indicated Russia used North Korean-made ballistic missiles to target energy infrastructure.

The bombardment struck critical transit and municipal assets, including Kyiv's 816-meter-long (892-yard-long) Northern Bridge, a vital artery over the Dnipro River connecting the city with major residential areas on the east bank. Russia's Ministry of Defense confirmed hitting the bridge, stating that it facilitated military hardware transportation. Moscow also claimed responsibility for hitting Kyiv's Meridian Plant—alleged to produce Flamingo cruise missiles—alongside an ammunition factory, explosives facilities, power substations, and a Black Sea cargo vessel.

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko described the energy, water, and heating situation as "extremely difficult." Emergency teams launched immediate repairs on the Northern Bridge, while Klitschko later confirmed that water pressure had been restored and roughly 60 percent of apartment buildings were reconnected to heating.

Describing the strike, 64-year-old Kyiv resident Kateryna Korniichuk told The Associated Press, as reported by ABC News:

“The first blast wave threw us against that wall and then against the other wall, because we were in the corridor. It’s very hard. No sleep, no peace — nothing. The children aren’t sleeping, and neither are we.”

Casualty Figures Disagree as Frontline Death Toll Rises

Discrepancies emerged among reporting outlets regarding casualties from Saturday's overnight strikes. DW reported that Ukrainian officials confirmed one person was killed and eight were injured in the attacks. Meanwhile, ABC News and BBC News reported nine people were wounded across Ukraine, including a child, with ABC News specifying that five individuals were injured in Kyiv.

Concurrently, a six-day search-and-rescue operation continued in the Zaporizhzhia region village of Komyshuvakha, where Russian guided bombs hit a former school and shelter on Monday while residents gathered for humanitarian aid. Emergency workers recovered nine additional bodies from the ruins over 24 hours, bringing the verified death toll to 55, with dozens still missing.

ABC News reported the account of 72-year-old resident Mykola Krasnov, who remained searching for his missing wife:

“She went to get humanitarian aid, and a three-ton bomb was dropped there. Where is she? She didn’t come home, and they didn't find her body there. She isn’t in the morgue, and she isn’t here alive.”

Energy Grid Damage Threatens Market Stability and Fuel Networks

The strikes targeted essential high-voltage electrical infrastructure just as winter conditions began to take hold. Ukraine's Energy Ministry reported that missile attacks damaged transmission networks carrying electricity from the Rivne and Khmelnytsky nuclear power plants to Kyiv and central Ukraine. Energy Minister Denys Shmyhal stated that power outages affected five regions, including severe disruptions in Kyiv, Zhytomyr, Vinnytsia, Rivne, Kharkiv, Donetsk, and Dnipropetrovsk. The Ministry noted these emergency blackouts were distinct from scheduled rolling blackouts.

In response, Ukrainian long-range strikes struck deep inside Russian territory, hitting key energy and military industrial assets. Russian media reported that a Ukrainian drone attack caused a major fire at the Saratov Oil Refinery in the Saratov region, following statements from Governor Roman Busargin regarding damaged civilian infrastructure.

Furthermore, Governor Andrey Klychkov of Russia's Oryol region acknowledged drone damage to commercial production sites, where media outlets reported a fire at the Proton-Electrotex plant, a producer of military equipment components. In the Belgorod region, local officials reported one civilian killed in Grayvoron by a Ukrainian drone, while Russia's Defense Ministry claimed its forces downed 411 Ukrainian drones overnight over 10 regions, Crimea, and surrounding seas. These escalating attacks on energy and industrial facilities highlight growing threats to regional infrastructure stability.

Pre-Talks Escalation and Diplomatic Maneuvers in Abu Dhabi

The intense military escalation comes immediately prior to trilateral peace talks scheduled for Tuesday in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. The upcoming meeting marks the fourth round of negotiations mediated by the administration of U.S. President Donald Trump, following talks in Geneva in February. Delegations from Ukraine, Russia, and the U.S. will seek a diplomatic path to end the war, though Western governments acknowledge deep impasses over territorial control and security guarantees.

President Zelenskyy accused Moscow of escalating attacks to build leverage before the talks, posting online that

"Russia perceives diplomacy in its own peculiar way – as a form of aggression,"
according to ABC News. Zelenskyy called for urgent U.S. bipartisan backing, greater support from Congress, and increased sanctions on Russia.

The Abu Dhabi meeting follows recent diplomatic activity in Washington. On Thursday, U.S. First Lady Melania Trump hosted Ukrainian and Russian delegations at the White House, calling for a "more enduring" ceasefire following a six-day local truce in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky that had enabled around 800 residents to evacuate, an intervention that both Moscow and Kyiv credited her with brokering.

Following that truce, President Donald Trump stated on Friday that the war could be near a settlement, envisioning a "big, beautiful agreement" with Moscow involving "all of Europe," DW reported. However, with severe combat continuing across Ukraine and Russia, diplomatic negotiators face severe challenges in bridging the divide.

Frequently Asked Questions

When and where are the upcoming Ukraine-Russia peace talks taking place?

Trilateral peace talks between Russia, Ukraine, and the United States are scheduled for Tuesday in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. This meeting marks the fourth round of negotiations brokered by the Trump administration, following previous talks in Geneva in February.

What damage did Russian airstrikes cause in Kyiv on November 7, 2026?

Overnight Russian missile and drone attacks damaged Kyiv's 816-meter Northern Bridge, residential homes, commercial properties, a kindergarten, and the Meridian Plant. The strikes damaged electrical substations connecting nuclear power plants to central Ukraine, creating severe power, heating, and water supply disruptions.

Did Ukraine launch counterattacks inside Russian territory?

Yes, Ukrainian drone strikes targeted multiple Russian regions overnight. Local officials and Russian media reported damage and fires at the Saratov Oil Refinery and the Proton-Electrotex plant in Oryol, while a drone strike in Grayvoron, Belgorod region, killed one person.

What was the local ceasefire in Oleshky and who brokered it?

A six-day local ceasefire in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky in Kherson allowed approximately 800 residents facing starvation and bombardment to evacuate. Both Moscow and Kyiv credited U.S. First Lady Melania Trump with brokering the temporary truce.

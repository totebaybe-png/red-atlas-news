Paraguay President Bank Collapse Triggers Impeachment, Arrest

By Red Atlas News Desk

Photo: Presidencia de la República del Ecuador / Public domain via Wikimedia Commons

Santiago Peña faces criminal charges as prosecutors seek pretrial detention following the sudden downfall of digital lender Ueno Bank.

On Saturday, November 7, 2026, prosecutors charged former Paraguayan President Santiago Peña with breach of trust and administration for personal gain following the paraguay president bank collapse of Ueno Bank. The Senate unanimously voted to impeach Peña on Friday, stripping him of office before prosecutors ordered his arrest and placed him under house arrest amid escalating street demonstrations across Asunción.

Key Facts

  • Unanimous Impeachment: Paraguay's Senate voted unanimously on Friday to remove President Santiago Peña from office following the failure of state-affiliated digital lender Ueno Bank.
  • Criminal Prosecution: Peña and former Central Bank Governor Carlos Carvallo face charges including breach of trust and administration for personal gain, with prosecutors seeking pretrial detention for both figures.
  • Millions Locked Out: The seizure of Ueno Bank by regulators on Tuesday froze savings and account access for nearly three million people, representing almost half of Paraguay's population of six million.
  • Pension Fund Mismanagement: Lawmakers accused Peña of improperly directing more than $800 million in public funds into Ueno Bank and depositing $355 million from the state pension fund beyond legal limits.
  • Political Succession: Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as president on Friday, though he also faces ongoing investigation regarding ties to the collapsed institution.

Senate Ousts Peña to Block Immunity After Ueno Seizure

The political downfall of Santiago Peña marks the most severe political disruption in Paraguay in over a decade. Congress moved rapidly after regulators stepped in to take control of Ueno Bank on Tuesday due to severe solvency concerns. Peña had submitted a formal letter of resignation on Thursday in an attempt to step down voluntarily, but lawmakers decisively rejected the offer. Congressional leaders insisted on pressing ahead with a formal impeachment trial to prevent Peña from retaining the political immunity granted to former presidents.

During the Senate proceedings, opposition members voiced intense anger over the government's handling of public funds and regulatory oversight. As reported by ABC News, opposition Senator Yolanda Paredes told reporters during the trial,

"He must go, but not home. He must go to a maximum-security prison. The public is fed up with impunity."
The Senate voted unanimously on Friday to strip Peña of the presidency.

Prior to his removal, Peña released a farewell statement acknowledging the severity of the crisis while maintaining his personal integrity. According to ABC News, Peña wrote,

"I do not want my presence to be a cause of greater division or instability. My responsibility is to put the country above my personal fate."
Peña, an ally of U.S. President Donald Trump, governed a landlocked nation of six million people that returned to democratic rule in 1989 and has been predominantly led by the conservative Colorado Party.

Criminal Charges and Questioning at the Attorney General's Office

Following the Senate's vote, law enforcement authorities acted quickly to issue arrest warrants for both Peña and former Central Bank chief Carlos Carvallo. Peña was initially placed under house arrest and subsequently transferred to a military base late Friday night as crowds of angry protesters began gathering outside his private residence, according to ABC News.

On Saturday morning, Peña was brought to the attorney general's office in Asunción for formal questioning. Aerial footage broadcast by local network Telefuturo showed the ousted president arriving at the compound inside an un-handcuffed state, wearing a navy blue pair of pants and a white shirt under heavy police escort. Later in the day, prosecutors officially announced charges against Peña, citing breach of trust and administration for personal gain, as reported by Reuters and Al Jazeera.

Carvallo was likewise charged on Saturday with breach of trust, producing false public documents, and administration for personal gain. Al Jazeera reported that prosecutors are formally asking the court to remand both Peña and Carvallo into pretrial detention while the broader criminal investigation unfolds.

Allegations of Insider Lending and Pension Fund Mismanagement

The core of the criminal case against Peña centers on his longstanding financial and political connections to Ueno Bank. The institution, a digital lender, experienced rapid expansion during Peña's presidency, bolstered by massive injections of public money totaling more than $800 million. Peña personally held a financial stake in Ueno's parent company until March 2025, according to Al Jazeera.

Investigators allege that Peña utilized his executive authority to steer public capital into Ueno Bank while actively insulating the firm from regulatory oversight despite growing warning signs concerning its high-risk insider lending practices. In 2023, Peña appointed Carvallo—a former executive at the enterprise that later became Ueno Bank—to serve as the head of Paraguay's central bank, creating what critics describe as an unmanageable conflict of interest.

Furthermore, prosecutors and lawmakers point to the illegal transfer of public capital into the lender. Peña is accused of authorizing Paraguay's state pension fund to deposit $355 million directly into Ueno Bank, an amount that exceeded established legal caps designed to protect national retirement reserves, according to Al Jazeera. Peña has consistently denied all criminal allegations, claiming that the bank's operational failures stemmed strictly from regulatory issues.

Financial System Shock and Deposit Reimbursement Efforts

The sudden collapse of Ueno Bank has delivered a major shock to Paraguay's financial sector. When central bank regulators seized control of the bank on Tuesday, nearly three million account holders—representing almost half the country's population—were instantly blocked from retrieving their funds or using debit cards for daily transactions.

To prevent widespread financial panic, the Central Bank of Paraguay stated on Saturday that approximately 1.2 million Ueno Bank customers had already received partial or full reimbursements of their insured deposits under statutory guarantees, as reported by Al Jazeera and ABC News. Despite these disbursements, more than 1.7 million depositors remain locked out of their accounts, maintaining high levels of public anxiety over the stability of the domestic financial ecosystem.

Street Protests, Union Strikes, and Political Succession

Public anger over the banking failure has manifested in violent street demonstrations throughout Asunción. Thousands of citizens demanding immediate access to their frozen savings have clashed with security forces. Police officers have deployed rubber bullets and pepper spray to disperse crowds surrounding government buildings, according to ABC News.

Following Peña's impeachment on Friday, Vice President Pedro Alliana was officially sworn in as president. Alliana, who belongs to the same conservative Colorado Party as Peña, pledged to expedite financial relief for affected citizens and began naming a new Cabinet on Saturday. However, Alliana's ascension has done little to quell public discontent, as Al Jazeera reported that the new president is himself facing investigative scrutiny regarding potential involvement in the Ueno Bank scandal.

Social tension is expected to escalate further early next week. Paraguay's largest teachers' union, representing more than 40,000 members, announced plans to initiate an indefinite national strike starting Monday to demand stronger legal protections for public pension funds, according to ABC News.

What Happens Next in Asunción

In the coming days, Paraguayan judicial authorities are scheduled to rule on prosecutors' requests for pretrial detention involving Peña and Carvallo. The central bank will continue processing deposit claims for the remaining 1.7 million Ueno Bank customers awaiting compensation.

Concurrently, newly sworn-in President Pedro Alliana faces immediate pressure as the national teachers' strike begins Monday, alongside growing demands from opposition lawmakers for broader investigations into the Colorado Party's political leadership.

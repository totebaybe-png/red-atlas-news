France Blames Iran for Supermarket Fridge Cyberattack

By Red Atlas News Desk

File photo: Quai d’Orsay @ Ministry of Foreign Affairs @ Paris. Photo: Guilhem Vellut from Paris, France / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

French officials claim state-backed actors targeted commercial refrigeration systems in an aggressive digital assault that caused fires and spoiled tons of food.

French authorities blamed Iran's paramilitary organization for digital assaults that disabled commercial cooling units throughout the nation last month, highlighting a major escalation in an active inquiry. The country's top diplomat, Jean-Noël Barrot, revealed the attribution during a media gathering at the international summit in Paris, confirming that officials had called in the Iranian diplomatic representative to the foreign ministry.

Key Facts

  • Target: Commercial refrigeration units across roughly 700 supermarkets and food stores in France.
  • Attribution: French spy and cyber agencies blamed Shahid Kaveh, a cyber offensive unit linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps.
  • Impact: Temperatures were overridden to reach over 50C, causing at least 15 fires and thousands of tons of spoiled food.
  • Response: The Iranian ambassador was summoned, and further EU sanctions were threatened by French officials.

French Officials Detail Modus Operandi and Shahid Kaveh Link

During the announcement, Paris public prosecutor Laure Beccuau stated that specialist investigators and government agencies identified a modus operandi characteristic of the Iranian group Shahid Kaveh. Beccuau linked the unit to the electronic and cyber center of the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps. Investigators recovered traces of Farsi and IP addresses traced back to devices located in Iran.

The Paris prosecutor's office opened an investigation against the head of Shahid Kaveh, his deputies, and others on charges of fraudulent attacks on an automated data processing system committed as part of an organized crime ring. The suspects named in the report alongside Shahid Kaveh include Hamid Reza Lashgarian, Mahdi Lashgarian, and Reza Salarvand. French officials stated that front groups such as CyberAveng3rs and Cyber Isnaad Front were used to mask the operations.

Economic Costs and Supermarket Devastation

The cyberattack struck hundreds of French supermarkets on October 11, overriding digital thermostats overnight. Retailers affected included Coopérative U, Intermarché, Netto, Carrefour, Monoprix, and E.Leclerc, targeting software provided by Danish engineering firm Danfoss. Some refrigerators had temperatures pushed so high they caught fire, triggering sprinkler systems and causing water damage.

Supermarket employees were forced to throw away entire inventories of perishable food, carting boxes of spoiled meat and melted ice cream into giant trash containers. One French supermarket owner estimated that the attack's total costs could add up to tens of millions of euros nationwide. A regional branch of France’s Crédit Mutuel bank also reported losing more than €100m in a separate cyber-attack on a Friday, though the bank stated it recuperated some of the funds.

Second Wave Hits European Blood Banks and Stores

The initial October incident was followed this month by a second wave of hacking that hit more than 100 establishments across Europe. Retailers in countries including the UK, Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Finland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and Sweden reported problems with refrigeration systems. The second wave also affected refrigerators used by French blood banks, prompting employees to move blood products to different cold storage units before destruction occurred.

Independent experts noted the scale of the disruption. Gérôme Billois, a partner at the cybersecurity and technology company Wavestone, stated according to The Guardian that Shahid Kaveh had been active since 2020 and was known for attacks on water distribution systems in the US. "Two countries are known for this kind of attack: Iran and Russia. Given that recent cyber-attacks on supermarkets and the blood bank in France concerned freezers it is reasonable to think Iran is directly linked to both," Billois said.

US Military Bases Face Similar Refrigerator Malfunctions

The sabotage of European supermarket fridges was preceded by at least one similar incident in the United States. In August, refrigerators in at least half a dozen military commissaries went out of commission. Pentagon investigators treated the malfunctions as a suspected cyberattack and worked with French officials to determine the cause.

A person familiar with the matter stated that the Pentagon considers Iran a suspect in the August commissary hacks, though the investigation remains in progress. The Pentagon stated that it was aware of speculation regarding a potential cyber incident but could not offer further comment for security reasons.

Next Steps and Diplomatic Measures

French officials stated that there would be consequences for Iran, with Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot threatening further European Union sanctions. Kaja Kallas, the head of foreign affairs for the EU, stated according to The Guardian: "We have the foreign affairs council next Monday, so we will have the opportunity to discuss this. These cyber incidents occurring in Europe, if attributed [to Iran], also call for a strong response on our part."

Additionally, experts have noted that ongoing geopolitical tensions, including the armed conflict between Washington and Tehran that commenced earlier in February, have generated an environment characterized by disparate digital disruptions. These occurrences involved prior network breaches directed at transit systems in Los Angeles during March, water facilities in July, and maritime vessels heading toward the United States in August. Despite these events, Iran's mission to the United Nations did not respond to inquiries for comment, maintaining its routine stance of denying involvement in malicious cyber operations.

The broader implications of these cyber incidents have also led to increased scrutiny over global cybersecurity vulnerabilities, particularly regarding obsolete equipment or outdated firmware running on commercial refrigeration units. Cybersecurity analysts pointed out that many affected systems relied on older software versions, which potentially lowered the barrier of entry for malicious actors seeking unauthorized access. As investigations continue across multiple jurisdictions, international cooperation remains critical in addressing these sophisticated state-sponsored cyber campaigns.

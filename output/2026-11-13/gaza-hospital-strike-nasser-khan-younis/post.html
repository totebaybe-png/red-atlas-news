Six people died and dozens were injured after two missiles struck the surgical unit at Khan Younis's largest medical facility.

An Israeli air strike on the Nasser Medical Complex in southern Gaza killed six people and injured up to 30 others overnight, according to health officials in the enclave. The Gaza hospital strike hit the facility's al-Yasin surgery building in Khan Younis around 00:30 local time, causing extensive damage to operating rooms, intensive care units, and sleeping quarters used by medical staff.

Key facts of the attack

  • Six people were killed and between 28 and 30 others were injured when missiles hit the surgical building at Nasser Hospital.
  • General surgeon Dr. Rizq Abu Shaira was among those killed, alongside patients and companions inside the facility.
  • Weapons experts identified unexploded ordinance at the scene as an American-made 250-pound precision-guided aerial bomb.
  • The Israeli military stated that the operation targeted Hamas Khan Younis Brigade commander Muhammad Abu Ismail, alleging the complex was used as a command headquarters.
  • Hamas denied operating from the hospital, describing the military's claims as false fabrications to cover up a war crime.

Survivors describe devastation inside the surgical wards

The strike hit a multi-story building within the hospital compound, penetrating the roof and detonating on the upper floors where medical staff were resting between surgical procedures. Dr. Ahmed al-Fara, general director of Nasser Hospital, stated during a news conference reported by the BBC that Israeli forces launched two missiles at the al-Yasin surgery building, though only one of the warheads exploded upon impact.

"What's happened in Nasser Medical Complex is targeting the health sector mainly. We are talking about doctors, we are talking about civilians, we are talking about patients," Dr. Ahmed al-Fara told a news conference reported by the BBC. He added that the attack caused severe structural damage to the hospital's internal infrastructure.

Dr. Mohammed Qdeih, a general surgery specialist who suffered head injuries in the blast, described being buried under falling masonry while lying near colleagues. "My whole body was trapped under the rubble. For a moment, I felt as if I was about to lose consciousness, or that I was about to die," Qdeih told the BBC. He described crawling through total darkness to free his leg from debris before discovering that Dr. Rizq Abu Shaira, a 52-year-old surgeon lying nearby, had been killed.

"Dr. Rizq was the kindest person in this department. He was very kind and always willing to help... And of course, he was a surgeon, one of the best among us," Qdeih told the BBC. He noted that several patients in adjacent rooms were also killed in the explosion. Footage of the aftermath showed that ceilings were shattered, metal frames were twisted, and debris was scattered across recovery beds and medical instruments.

Munitions analysis confirms American-made aerial bomb

Footage recorded inside the building and distributed by The Associated Press showed the tail section and casing of an unexploded munition lying inside the damaged surgical department. Independent weapons analysts evaluated the video evidence to determine the type of armaments utilized in the bombardment.

Trevor Ball, a former explosive ordnance disposal technician for the U.S. Army and current analyst at Armament Research Services, told The New York Times that the video footage showed two 250-pound precision-guided aerial bombs manufactured in the United States. Ball observed that one bomb penetrated the roof and multiple floors of the structure but failed to detonate, leaving its warhead intact with visible serial numbers.

Conflicting claims surrounding the gaza nasser hospital strike

The Israeli military and the Shin Bet security agency issued a joint statement confirming the strike, claiming it aimed to eliminate senior militant leadership operating from inside the facility. The military identified its primary target as Muhammad Abu Ismail, the commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade, whom Israeli authorities accused of holding hostages captive during the war and planning attacks against Israeli troops.

An Israeli military spokeswoman, speaking anonymously to The New York Times, confirmed that another victim, Khaled Abu Anza, was a Hamas operative. The Israeli military claimed it had established a command center inside the hospital complex and stored weaponry there. Israeli officials released an audio recording of a telephone conversation that they said demonstrated an advance warning given to a Gaza health official prior to the attack. However, Dr. Fara denied that hospital administration received any warnings before the missiles hit.

Hamas rejected the Israeli assertions regarding a nasser hospital hamas presence. In a public statement reported by The New York Times, the group called the Israeli explanation a "blatant attempt to justify a heinous crime targeting a civilian medical facility." While residents in Gaza have acknowledged that personnel linked to the civil government sometimes occupy offices in medical complexes, Hamas denied that military combat operations were conducted from Nasser Hospital.

Pattern of strikes on healthcare infrastructure

Thursday's attack marks the seventh time that the Israeli military has struck Nasser Hospital since the conflict began. The facility is the largest remaining operational medical center in southern Gaza, serving a regional population of nearly one million residents. In August 2025, a double strike on the same hospital complex killed 20 to 22 people, including healthcare personnel and journalists. That incident prompted Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to publicly acknowledge a "tragic mishap," as reported by The New York Times.

The World Health Organization (WHO) reported that it has documented 1,056 attacks on healthcare facilities and personnel across Gaza between October 2023 and late 2026. According to WHO figures, those attacks resulted in 1,010 deaths and 1,725 injuries among medical workers, patients, and displaced civilians.

"Gaza's health facilities are lifelines for its people. They must be protected. They must never be militarized or attacked," WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus warned in a statement reported by the BBC. International humanitarian law provides special protection to hospitals and medical staff, though facilities can forfeit protection if used to commit acts harmful to an enemy force, subject to specific warning procedures.

Ceasefire strain and worsening humanitarian crisis

The strike comes more than a year into a U.S.-brokered ceasefire signed by Israel and Hamas in October 2025. Despite the formal agreement, military operations have continued frequently across the Gaza Strip. According to Gaza's health ministry, more than 1,620 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli fire since the ceasefire took effect. Over the same period, Israel reported that six of its soldiers were killed in Gaza.

The total reported death toll in Gaza since October 7, 2023, stands at more than 74,650 people, according to local health officials. The conflict began when Hamas-led combatants launched an attack on southern Israel, killing approximately 1,200 people and taking 251 hostages.

Humanitarian agencies report that living conditions for Gaza's two million residents have deteriorated further due to seasonal winter weather. Heavy rainfall over the preceding weekend flooded tens of thousands of temporary tents housing displaced families across central and southern Gaza, destroying personal shelter while sanitation systems remain heavily damaged.

What happens next

Following the strike on Nasser Hospital, additional Israeli attacks were reported across Gaza on Thursday. Medics reported that four more Palestinians died in separate strikes targeting Gaza City, Jabalia, Deir al-Balah, and the Bureij refugee camp. The Israeli military confirmed strikes on individual targets in Deir al-Balah and Gaza City.

News agencies published photographs showing what appeared to be an unlocated unexploded missile inside the facility following the bombardment. The World Health Organization reiterated its demand for immediate, unhindered humanitarian access to deliver emergency medical supplies and fuel to keep southern Gaza's last major hospital operating.

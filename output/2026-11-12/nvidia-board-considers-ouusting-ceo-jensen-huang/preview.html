Nvidia Board Meets to Decide Future of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang

By Red Atlas News Desk

File photo: NVIDIA headquarters at 2788-2888 San Tomas Expressway in Santa Clara, California.. Photo: Coolcaesar / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons

The chipmaker's directors held emergency talks after reports of an affair with a direct subordinate compounded existing security probes into a Chinese businesswoman.

SAN JOSE, Calif. — The board of directors at Nvidia met Wednesday evening to evaluate whether to remove co-founder and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang from his role, following reports detailing two extramarital relationships that have wiped out roughly $600 billion of the technology company's market capitalization this week.

The emergency board meeting followed a report by The Wall Street Journal stating that directors were preparing to oust the 63-year-old executive. Under company bylaws, Nvidia's 10 independent directors can remove Huang through a majority vote, with or without cause. Huang controls approximately 3.6% of Nvidia's stock and has led the $4 trillion semiconductor giant since its establishment in 1993.

Key facts surrounding the board review

  • Nvidia's board convened on Wednesday night to discuss removing CEO Jensen Huang following disclosures of two separate personal relationships.
  • Shares of Nvidia declined by nearly 9% in pre-market trading, extending weekly market value losses to $640 billion.
  • Law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz is conducting an independent investigation into Huang's relationship with a Chinese businesswoman accused by U.S. intelligence of being a foreign spy asset.
  • A second relationship involving Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, directly violated company rules governing internal conflicts of interest.
  • External executive search firms have been engaged to evaluate internal and external successor candidates, including Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress.

Allegations involving Nvidia ceo Jensen Huang kimberly powell

Discussions regarding Huang's position accelerated after The New York Times reported on Wednesday that he engaged in an extramarital relationship with a direct subordinate. Five former Nvidia employees told the newspaper that the executive maintained a relationship with Kimberly Powell, the company's 48-year-old vice president of healthcare.

Powell joined Nvidia in 2008 and was promoted to lead its healthcare division in 2017. Both Huang and Powell are married to other partners. According to people familiar with the incident, the two were seen displaying affection at a San Jose hotel during Nvidia's GTC developer conference in March. During that same event, Powell delivered an artificial intelligence presentation that featured cartoon illustrations of Huang.

Three people informed about the March incident told The New York Times that after a bystander recorded video footage of the interaction on a smartphone, members of Huang's personal security detail intercepted the individual and requested that they sign a non-disclosure agreement. The newspaper reported that it had not viewed the recording.

Nvidia's corporate code of conduct requires employees to refrain from personal relationships that create internal conflicts of interest and mandates prompt corporate investigations into reported violations. When contacted by telephone by The New York Times on Wednesday, Huang declined to comment on the matter, stating, "I don't think you should be talking to me directly," before disconnecting the call.

Espionage probe involving Nvidia ceo Jensen Huang alice huang

The disclosures regarding Powell follow revelations earlier in the week concerning Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a senior executive at Singapore-based data center operator Megaspeed. Despite sharing a surname, the two individuals are not related. The Wall Street Journal reported that the pair were photographed together in Taipei in both 2024 and 2025.

According to reports, U.S. intelligence agencies circulated a document earlier this year alleging that Alice Huang operated as an asset for China's Ministry of State Security, assisting in diverting advanced Nvidia processors to Chinese firms. China has rejected the espionage allegations. On Sunday, Nvidia's board announced it had retained law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead an independent probe into whether foreign intelligence targeted Huang.

In a memo distributed to Nvidia staff on Sunday, Huang acknowledged unfaithfulness to his wife, writing, "I was unfaithful to my wife," and taking full responsibility without detailing the relationship. In a concurrent statement, Huang wrote: "I support the investigation and will cooperate fully. I love our country and our company."

Republican leaders across three U.S. House of Representatives committees have called for Huang to attend a closed-door congressional briefing regarding his contact with the alleged foreign agent. Additionally, Nvidia shareholders have filed a class-action lawsuit against Huang and the corporation, asserting that management withheld material personal information from investors.

Global stock market decline and economic fallout

News of the potential executive change led to sell-offs across international equity markets. In extended trading on Wednesday, Nvidia shares dropped by as much as 8.8%, erasing $360 billion in market value in a single session and bringing total weekly losses to $640 billion, or 14% of the company's total capitalization. In Frankfurt pre-market trading on Thursday, shares opened down 7.2%.

The drop reverberated across Asian trading floors. Taiwan's benchmark Taiex index fell 2.6%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng index declined 1.3%. In Japan, the Nikkei 225 fell 0.7%, South Korea's Kospi lost 0.6%, and mainland China's Shenzhen Composite dropped 1.6%. In Europe, the STOXX Europe 600 Technology index fell 1.3%, pulled down by declines in chip suppliers ASML, which fell 2.5% in Amsterdam, and STMicroelectronics, which lost 2.6% in Paris.

Futures for the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 dropped 0.9%, while S&P 500 futures fell 0.4%. Energy markets also moved, with Brent crude rising 0.55% to $93.27 per barrel, continuing fluctuations between $72 and $120 since conflict involving Iran escalated.

Market analysts cited the sudden nature of the board review as a primary driver of investor anxiety. "One of the most impressive careers is set to end," said Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG. "Huang might have been able somehow to cling on at Nvidia after one revelation (though news about chip shipments made that doubtful), but after two, one of which involving a direct report, his fate is sealed."

Potential successor candidates for the chief executive role

Because Huang maintains centralized oversight over Nvidia's daily operations with dozens of direct reports, industry analysts note that the organization lacks a single designated heir. Ben Barringer, head of technology research at Quilter Cheviot, noted that the board has taken severe action. "Clearly Nvidia's board is taking the allegations of another affair by Jensen Huang incredibly seriously given they have gone as far as hiring an external search firm to look for potential successors," Barringer said.

Barringer highlighted Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as the most prominent internal candidate to take over leadership, though he noted Huang might remain in a diminished governance role. "There is no natural successor at Nvidia, although CFO Colette Kress would be the most obvious choice," Barringer said, adding that "there is every possibility he stays on as president in a much reduced role, but retaining some level of influence."

Other internal managers cited by technology commentators include Ian Buck, who directs data center client operations, and Bryan Catanzano, vice president of applied deep learning research. Family members within the firm have also been discussed by market analysts, including Huang's daughter Madison Huang, senior director of product marketing, and his son Spencer Huang, director of product management in robotics. AMD Chief Executive Lisa Su, who is Huang's cousin, has also been mentioned alongside external figures such as Arm Chief Executive Rene Haas and former Apple Chief Executive Tim Cook, who retired from Apple in August 2026.

Next steps for Nvidia board and regulatory oversight

The board of directors is expected to complete its formal vote on Huang's employment status following the conclusion of its emergency meeting. Meanwhile, independent investigators from Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz are continuing their inquiry into security vulnerabilities related to chip diversion.

In Washington, federal oversight is intensifying. Axios reported Thursday that U.S. President Donald Trump authorized a third crackdown this year on entities suspected of routing Nvidia AI chips to China, acting after discussions with Elon Musk. Additionally, congressional committee leaders are setting dates for Huang's requested closed-door testimony, while federal courts begin processing the newly filed shareholder class-action lawsuit.

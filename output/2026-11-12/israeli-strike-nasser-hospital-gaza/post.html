The attack on southern Gaza's largest medical facility destroyed surgical wards, killed health workers and patients, and prompted fresh calls for ceasefire enforcement.

An Israeli airstrike hit the Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis early Thursday morning, killing at least six people and injuring up to 28 others, according to Palestinian health officials and medical workers. The overnight attack struck a five-storey building containing surgical wards and intensive care units around 12:30 a.m. Hospital director Dr. Ahmed al-Fara reported that the strike caused severe structural damage and forced the complete suspension of vital services across four key divisions, including radiology, laboratories, surgical operations, and inpatient surgical wards. Nasser Hospital is the largest operational health facility in southern Gaza, serving an estimated one million residents.

Casualties and Eyewitness Accounts

Among the six people killed in the strike was Dr. Rizq Abu Shaira, a 52-year-old general surgeon who had recently been featured in the 2026 documentary American Doctor alongside visiting foreign physicians. Medical staff confirmed that Dr. Abu Shaira was killed alongside a patient he had operated on. Another victim was identified as 29-year-old Seif Ghazi Abu Hatab, a patient admitted earlier in the week who was scheduled for surgery later Thursday morning. According to his cousin, Tareq Abu Hatab, Seif had already lost his father and two brothers during the war. Local officials and medics noted that the dead also included a 13-year-old boy, a disabled patient, and a man identified as Khaled Abu Anza.

Surviving medical personnel described chaotic scenes inside the facility immediately following the blast. Dr. Ayman Abu Ubaid, a surgical specialist who suffered injuries in the attack, stated that he was resting during an overnight shift when an explosion destroyed the room. He recalled thick smoke filling the hallways, fires breaking out, and widespread debris trapping patients and staff. Dr. Mohammed Qdeih, a general surgery specialist who sustained head wounds, reported being buried under rubble near Dr. Abu Shaira before managing to pull himself free in total darkness. Medics reported that most of the injured suffered shrapnel wounds and severe limb trauma.

Munitions Analysis and Structural Damage

Video footage collected from inside the damaged surgery department showed extensive destruction, twisted medical equipment, caved-in ceilings, and a large hole where munitions penetrated the structure. Analysis of the footage by Trevor Ball, a former U.S. Army explosive-ordnance disposal technician working for Armament Research Services, indicated that the attack was carried out using two 250-pound American-manufactured precision-guided aerial bombs. Munition fragments and an unexploded warhead bearing a visible serial number were filmed inside the facility, confirming reports from hospital leadership that one of the two launched missiles failed to detonate upon impact.

Military Statements and Conflicting Claims

The Israeli military and security services released a joint statement confirming the strike, asserting that it was a precise operation targeting Mohammed Abu Ismail, whom they identified as the commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade responsible for keeping hostages captive. Israeli military officials claimed that Hamas fighters had converted the hospital complex into an operational headquarters, using it to store weapons and direct attacks against Israeli troops. Additionally, an Israeli military spokesperson identified victim Khaled Abu Anza as a Hamas operative. While the Israeli military stated it issued advance warnings—releasing an audio recording of an alleged warning call to a Gaza health official—Dr. al-Fara firmly denied that the hospital received any notice prior to the bombardment. Hamas rejected the military's claims of operating from the facility, characterizing the allegations as a baseless attempt to cover up a war crime against a protected civilian medical facility.

Impact on Healthcare Infrastructure and International Law

Under international humanitarian law, medical facilities maintain protected status unless they are being utilized for military operations. The latest attack marks the seventh time the Nasser Hospital complex has been targeted by Israeli forces during the war, including an August strike that killed over 20 people, among them journalists and medical personnel. Gaza's health infrastructure has been severely degraded, with UN experts estimating that 90% of facilities have been damaged or destroyed—a pattern of destruction they termed "medicide." Furthermore, more than 1,700 healthcare workers have been killed in Gaza, while Doctors Without Borders (MSF) reports that Israel has detained hundreds of medics since October 7, 2023, with at least 95 still in custody. The World Health Organization (WHO) documented 1,056 attacks on Gaza healthcare facilities over three years, resulting in 1,010 fatalities. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus emphasized that hospitals are critical lifelines that must never be militarized or attacked.

Wider Military Operations across Gaza

Beyond the strike on Nasser Hospital, Israeli military operations across Gaza resulted in additional fatalities on Thursday. Medics reported that an airstrike targeting a vehicle in Gaza City killed two people and wounded several others, while another individual was killed by Israeli gunfire at the Bureij refugee camp in central Gaza. Further strikes were reported in Jabalia and Deir al-Balah. The Israeli military confirmed conducting targeted strikes against suspects in Gaza City and Deir al-Balah, bringing the territory's total death toll for Thursday to at least nine people.

Ceasefire Status and Diplomatic Reactions

The strikes occurred despite a US-brokered ceasefire agreement announced in October 2025 between Israel and Hamas. The Board of Peace, an oversight body established under U.S. President Donald Trump's peace framework, issued a statement on Thursday calling for the immediate implementation of the peace plan without delay in light of the Nasser Hospital casualties. According to the Gaza health ministry, more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli forces since the ceasefire was declared. The broader conflict, initiated by the Hamas-led assault on southern Israel on October 7, 2023—which resulted in approximately 1,200 Israeli deaths and 251 hostages taken—has claimed more than 74,600 Palestinian lives in Gaza, according to health ministry figures. Israel reports six of its soldiers have been killed in Gaza during the ceasefire period.

Worsening Storms and Humanitarian Crisis

The military escalation coincides with a rapidly deteriorating humanitarian environment brought on by severe weather. Heavy seasonal rainfall last week flooded emergency camps across the enclave, where roughly 65% of the population resides in tents or temporary shelters. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reported that over 26,000 households were impacted by severe storms as of November 8. The flooding previously caused a sandy embankment to collapse, killing five displaced people, including three children. On Thursday, a nine-month-old infant, Kinan Ahmed Raqiq, died of hypothermia inside a displacement tent in Khan Younis. His father, Ahmed Raqiq, recounted rushing the blue-skinned infant to Nasser Hospital, where doctors confirmed he had frozen to death. UNICEF spokesman James Elder warned that Gaza is experiencing unprecedented cold and rain weeks before the formal start of winter, compounding the suffering of displaced families.

Frequently Asked Questions

What occurred during the strike on Nasser Hospital in Gaza?

Early on Thursday, an Israeli air attack struck a five-storey surgical building at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis with two 250-pound precision-guided bombs, one of which did not explode. The attack killed at least six people, wounded up to 28, and damaged critical departments including surgery, radiology, and intensive care.

What reason did the Israeli military give for the hospital strike?

The Israeli military stated that it targeted Mohammed Abu Ismail, identified as the commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade, along with other fighters. The military alleged that Hamas was using the hospital complex as a headquarters to store weapons and direct attacks against its forces.

How have Gaza officials and international bodies responded to the strike?

Hospital director Dr. Ahmed al-Fara and Hamas denied that the facility was used for military operations or that advance warnings were received, calling the strike a war crime. The World Health Organization and UN officials reiterated that healthcare facilities are protected under international law and must not be targeted.

How many casualties have occurred in Gaza since the ceasefire agreement?

According to the Gaza health ministry, more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli fire since the US-brokered ceasefire was agreed upon in October 2025. Israel has reported six soldier fatalities in Gaza during the same timeframe.

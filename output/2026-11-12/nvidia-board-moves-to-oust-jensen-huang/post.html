The co-founder faces potential removal following allegations of undisclosed workplace and foreign relationships, sending tech stocks lower.

The board of directors at Nvidia is preparing to oust Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang following revelations of multiple extramarital relationships and an ongoing probe into potential foreign intelligence targeting, according to reports published Wednesday by The Wall Street Journal and The New York Times. The decision to consider removing the long-serving chief executive sent Nvidia shares down as much as 9% in after-hours trading, triggering widespread declines across connected technology stocks and deepening a multi-day selloff in the semiconductor sector.

  • Board deliberations: The Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia's board is preparing to meet imminently to decide whether to push out co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang.
  • Executive relationship: The New York Times reported a direct affair between Huang and Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, citing five former employees and video footage from a San Jose hotel.
  • Espionage investigation: The board previously retained law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to examine Huang's acknowledged affair with Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence officials identified as an asset for China's Ministry of State Security.
  • Market impact: Nvidia lost more than $427 billion in market capitalization earlier in the week, with after-hours trading on Wednesday dragging down suppliers and ecosystem partners including TSMC, Supermicro, Micron, and CoreWeave.
  • Succession planning: Bloomberg reported that the company is working with an executive search firm to evaluate internal and external candidates, including the potential appointment of an interim chief executive.

Board moves to oust Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang after internal relationship revealed

Reports detailing the board's preparation to oust Huang emerged late Wednesday, minutes after The New York Times disclosed details of a relationship between Huang and an executive who reports directly to him. According to the report, which cited five unnamed former employees, Huang engaged in an extramarital affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare. The New York Times reported that the pair were recorded on camera behaving affectionately with one another at a hotel in San Jose, California, in March.

Powell has led Nvidia's healthcare division since 2017. Her corporate biography states that she oversees worldwide hardware and software platforms for accelerated computing, artificial intelligence, visualization, medical imaging, life sciences, drug discovery, and healthcare analytics. A former employee speaking on condition of anonymity noted that rumors regarding a relationship between Huang and Powell had circulated internally for years, partly because Powell lacked a traditional background in science or medicine.

Healthcare remains a smaller business unit within Nvidia compared to its primary revenue engines in graphics processing units and large-scale artificial intelligence hardware. While Powell has frequently represented Nvidia at public events—including the firm's annual GTC conference—to highlight AI applications in drug discovery and clinical efficiency, the company's core financial growth has relied on hardware supplied to hyperscale data centers.

Neither Nvidia nor Powell responded to requests for comment following the reports on Wednesday. Heller, a crisis public relations firm retained by Huang as early as last year, also did not immediately respond to inquiries.

Counterintelligence probe targets executive ties to alleged Chinese agent

The impending board meeting follows a separate admission by Huang on Sunday, when he acknowledged having an extramarital affair in a company-wide memo. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did," Huang wrote in the message to Nvidia employees, as reported by CNBC. "I am deeply sorry."

While Huang did not name his partner in the Sunday memo, The Financial Times and The New York Times subsequently reported that U.S. intelligence officials believe the woman, identified as Alice Huang, is a spy working for China's Ministry of State Security. Alice Huang, a Chinese national who is not related to Jensen Huang, served as an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing provider that purchases hardware from Nvidia.

Days prior to his public apology, Huang told Bloomberg that his interactions with Alice Huang were related exclusively to legitimate business dealings and were "all completely normal and not unusual." However, the allegations prompted Nvidia's board of directors to initiate an independent investigation to determine whether corporate operations or sensitive technology had been compromised.

The board hired Wall Street law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to head the inquiry, selecting partner Joon H. Kim, a former U.S. attorney, to lead the probe. The board publicly stated it had found no evidence that national security or trade secrets were compromised, but lawmakers responded by dispatching a formal letter to Huang requesting his direct testimony before Congress, citing "significant national security concerns."

Market capitalization falls as Wall Street issues analyst downgrades

News of the board's potential action against Huang reverberated across global financial markets, driving Nvidia shares down by 8% to more than 9% in extended trading on Wednesday. The decline extended a broader retreat that wiped out more than $427 billion in Nvidia's market capitalization over the first two trading sessions following Sunday's disclosure.

The selloff spread rapidly to corporations with financial and supply chain connections to Nvidia. In after-hours trading, shares of server manufacturer Supermicro fell nearly 5%, while cloud provider CoreWeave—in which Nvidia is an investor—dropped more than 4%. Memory chip supplier Micron declined nearly 3%, and primary semiconductor foundry TSMC lost over 2%.

Wall Street firms reacted by stripping Nvidia of key investment ratings. Baird downgraded Nvidia's stock to neutral, citing leadership uncertainty, potential softening in customer demand, and limited visibility into 2027 data center spending plans. Morgan Stanley removed Nvidia from its "Top Pick" list while maintaining an overweight rating, and Bank of America Global Research pulled the company from its "U.S. 1 List."

The executive crisis coincides with existing pressures on the technology sector. Nvidia stock was already down 7% earlier in the week, heading toward its sixth consecutive weekly decline. Market sentiment has been weighed down by questions over the long-term sustainability of artificial intelligence infrastructure spending, exacerbated by recent U.S. midterm election results in which Democratic majorities swept both houses of Congress alongside state ballot measures that could restrict data center construction.

Governance structures and succession options under review

Jensen Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has served as its chief executive for over three decades, guiding its evolution from a niche gaming graphics supplier into a dominant provider of artificial intelligence hardware. His long tenure has left the corporate culture heavily reliant on his leadership, with customers and competitors routinely referring to the executive simply as "Jensen."

Nvidia has not publicly released a formal succession plan. On Sunday, following Huang's initial admission, the board told Business Insider that "Jensen is committed to continuing to lead Nvidia as CEO without interruption." However, reporting by Bloomberg on X indicated that the board is now working alongside an executive search firm to identify candidates for both an interim leadership role and a permanent replacement.

Industry analysts and former company employees have identified internal executives as potential candidates to lead the firm. These include Ian Buck, vice president of accelerated computing, and Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer. Huang's two adult children, Madison and Spencer, also work at Nvidia in operational roles.

Huang's departure would also highlight unique corporate control structures. The majority of Huang's equity in Nvidia—approximately 800 million shares—is co-owned with his wife, Lori Huang, and held within family trusts. Huang directly holds roughly 70 million shares on his own, giving him limited individual economic control over the company despite his prominent public profile.

Board meeting scheduled as independent investigation proceeds

Nvidia's board of directors is scheduled to meet as early as Wednesday evening to deliberate on Huang's position and determine whether to appoint an interim chief executive. Meanwhile, the independent investigation led by Joon H. Kim of Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz continues to review communication records and security protocols surrounding Huang's interactions with Alice Huang. The chief executive also faces an upcoming deadline to respond to congressional requests for formal testimony regarding his contacts with foreign tech executives.

Which Gold Purity to Buy in New Zealand: 24K (9999 Fine), 22K, 18K, 14K, and 9K

In mainstream New Zealand high-street jewellery stores, 18K gold (stamped 750) and 9K gold (stamped 375) are the dominant choices, alongside 14K gold (stamped 585). While 18K is favoured for luxury engagement rings and fine gemstone jewellery, 9K gold is an everyday Kiwi staple that offers high durability and scratch resistance at an accessible retail price.

New Zealand GST Exemption on Fine Investment Gold vs. Retail Jewellery

One of the biggest advantages for savers buying physical bullion in New Zealand is the tax treatment under the Goods and Services Tax (GST) Act. In New Zealand, fine gold with a purity of at least 99.5% (which includes all 24K 999 and 9999 investment gold bars and pure bullion coins) is classified as a financial service and is completely exempt from GST.

However, buyers must pay close attention to purity when selecting coins. The Inland Revenue Department (IRD) threshold for GST-free gold in New Zealand is strictly 99.5% fineness. That means 24K (9999 fine) coins like the Australian Kangaroo, Canadian Maple Leaf, and modern 24K Britannia are GST-free, whereas 22K gold coins (such as the South African Krugerrand, American Gold Eagle, or British Sovereign, which are 91.67% pure) fall below the 99.5% fine gold threshold and can attract New Zealand GST unless purchased through specific second-hand dealer channels.

All finished gold jewellery sold at retail in New Zealand—whether 9K, 14K, 18K, 22K, or 24K wearable ornaments—is treated as a standard consumer good and includes New Zealand GST in the retail price.

  • Gold bullion bars and coins with a purity of at least 99.5% (all 24K 999 and 9999 products) are exempt from GST in New Zealand.
  • 24K coins like the Australian Kangaroo and Canadian Maple Leaf are GST-free across New Zealand.
  • Lower-purity 22K coins (91.67% fine) fall below the 99.5% fine metal threshold and may attract GST when sold new.
  • All retail gold jewellery sold in New Zealand shops includes standard GST on top of craftsmanship markups.

What Moves the Gold Price in New Zealand: The Kiwi Dollar, RBNZ, and Global Trends

The gold price in New Zealand dollars is shaped by two constant drivers: the international US dollar spot price of bullion and the exchange value of the New Zealand dollar (the Kiwi). Because the Kiwi dollar is a small, open, agricultural-export currency sensitive to global risk sentiment, it often weakens against the US dollar during global financial downturns or risk-off episodes. That currency depreciation automatically lifts the NZD gold price, making physical gold a strong portfolio hedge for New Zealand households.

Monetary policy decisions by the Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) also influence local demand. When the Official Cash Rate (OCR) is lowered or domestic inflation erodes the real return on bank term deposits, New Zealand savers frequently allocate capital into physical bullion and safe-haven assets.

At the same time, global macroeconomic trends—including central bank gold purchases, sovereign debt levels, geopolitical tensions, and US interest rate cycles—set the overarching direction for precious metals worldwide. Watching the live NZD chart directly ensures you see the combined net impact of both global bullion moves and Kiwi dollar shifts.

  • Movements in the NZD/USD exchange rate immediately affect the New Zealand dollar gold price throughout the day.
  • Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) Official Cash Rate decisions and domestic inflation impact local investor demand.
  • A weaker Kiwi dollar during global market volatility often boosts the NZD gold price even when USD gold is steady.
  • Global central bank reserve buying and safe-haven demand drive the long-term bullion trend.

Understanding 24K, 22K, 21K, and 18K Gold Purities in Pakistan

Pakistani bullion dealers and retail jewellers categorize gold using both karat ratings and traditional local terminology. At the pinnacle sits 24K gold (999 fineness), commonly referred to in local wholesale markets as Pasha or pure Biscuit gold. Because 24 carat metal is exceedingly soft and prone to bending, it is traded almost exclusively in the form of investment bars, ary biscuits, and Swiss-minted wafers rather than wearable jewellery.

For bridal sets, heavy necklaces, and traditional kangan or kara bangles, 22K gold (916 fineness) and 21K gold (875 fineness) dominate Pakistani showrooms. While 22K gold price Pakistan today is the top benchmark for high-grade bridal ornaments in major urban boutiques, 21 carat gold is equally widespread across many regional bazaars because its slightly higher alloy content provides extra durability for intricate hand-worked designs and everyday wear.

At the more durable end of the showcase, 18K gold (750 fineness) is widely chosen for contemporary lightweight jewellery, gemstone rings, and diamond-mounted pieces where rigid prongs are essential to prevent stone loss. Because some traditional shops use local terms like pathor alongside karat numbers, buyers should always insist on clear karat confirmation—whether 24K, 22K, 21K, or 18K—and compare the exact column in our live table above.

  • 24K (999 fineness): Pure bullion known locally as Pasha or Biscuit gold, ideal for pure investment.
  • 22K (916 fineness): Premier bridal jewellery standard offering rich yellow colour and high resale value.
  • 21K (875 fineness): Widely traded standard across regional Pakistani sarafa markets for durable ornaments.
  • 18K (750 fineness): Preferred alloy for modern stone-set rings, bracelets, and daily-wear pendants.

Traditional Pakistani Gold Weights: Tola, Masha, Ratti, and Grams

Pakistan preserves one of the richest traditional weight systems in the global bullion trade. The cornerstone of the market is the tola, which equals 11.66 grams (more precisely 11.6638 grams) on a digital metric scale. In the classic subcontinental system still spoken fluently by every Pakistani goldsmith, one tola is subdivided into twelve mashas, and each masha is further divided into eight rattis. Even today, when families commission a wedding set or weigh an heirloom locket, conversations naturally blend tolas and mashas with metric grams.

alongside the 1 tola benchmark, Pakistan's wholesale bullion associations officially publish rates per 10 grams to align with modern digital scales. Because 10 grams is slightly lighter than 1 tola (11.66 grams), buyers must be careful never to confuse the two figures when comparing quotes between different shops or online charts. Our price-by-weight table above displays both 10 grams and 1 tola side by side so you can convert between them without a calculator.

In retail showrooms, every transaction is weighed on an electronic balance calibrated to fractions of 1 gram. Knowing how traditional units map onto metric grams protects you from rounding errors, especially when a jeweller discusses minor alloy deductions or wastage in terms of rattis per tola.

  • 1 Tola (11.66 grams): The primary traditional unit for quoting gold bullion and bridal sets in Pakistan.
  • Masha and Ratti: Traditional subdivisions of a tola (twelve mashas per tola; eight rattis per masha).
  • 10 Grams: Standard metric wholesale unit published alongside the tola by sarafa associations.
  • 1 Gram: The base metric unit displayed on electronic showroom scales for exact ornament billing.

Making Charges (Mazdoori), Polish, and Ratti Deductions Explained

When buying gold jewellery in Pakistan, the price per tola or per gram is only the starting component of your invoice. Jewellers add craftsmanship labour fees—known locally as mazdoori or making charges—to cover the artisan's time, workshop fuel, and design intricacy. Simple machine-drawn chains or plain kara bangles attract relatively modest mazdoori per gram, whereas elaborate hand-crafted bridal sets, antique jhumkas, and kundan or meenakari pieces command substantially higher labour charges.

Another vital concept in Pakistani jewellery shops is the polish or ratti deduction system, sometimes called katoti upon resale or exchange. When selling old ornaments back to a jeweller, shops routinely test purity and may deduct a small weight allowance—often expressed as a couple of rattis per tola—to account for solder joints, copper alloy content, or refining loss, especially if the item was purchased from a different jeweller without a stamped purity guarantee.

Furthermore, if an ornament contains artificial stones, zircon, pearls, lac (lakh) filling inside hollow bangles, or glass beads, you should verify whether the shop is charging the gold rate on the gross weight or strictly on the net gold weight. Reputable jewellers clearly separate net gold mass from stone and lac weight on the receipt and write a clear buyback guarantee specifying the exact deduction policy.

  • Mazdoori (making charges) depends on craftsmanship complexity, from basic chains to heavy bridal work.
  • Ask the jeweller to deduct the weight of zircon, stones, pearls, and inner lac filling before billing.
  • Understand katoti or ratti deductions in advance—insist on a written buyback guarantee on your receipt.
  • Returning an ornament to the original issuing jeweller usually secures the highest resale valuation.

What Drives the Gold Price in Pakistan: PKR Exchange Rate, Inflation, and Weddings

Two powerful forces govern the trajectory of the Pakistan gold rate today: international spot bullion movements and the value of the Pakistani rupee. Because global gold is priced in US dollars, any depreciation of the PKR against the dollar immediately amplifies the local price per tola, even if international bullion remains completely unchanged. For this reason, Pakistani savers closely watch currency exchange trends alongside global economic headlines.

Domestically, persistent consumer inflation and limited alternative savings avenues drive steady household demand for physical bullion. Across urban and rural Pakistan alike, families view 24K biscuits and 22K ornaments as a tangible, Sharia-aligned store of value that preserves purchasing power across generations and can be pledged or liquidated in any bazaar within minutes.

Seasonal cultural calendars also create predictable surges in showroom traffic. The peak Pakistani wedding (shaadi) seasons—particularly following Eid-ul-Fitr, Eid-ul-Adha, and throughout the cooler winter months—generate intense demand for bridal jewellery sets, gold coins, and gift bangles, often widening retail making charges and tightening physical bullion supply in local markets.

  • PKR-to-USD exchange rate movements have an immediate, direct impact on local per-tola prices.
  • Households rely on physical gold as a primary hedge against domestic inflation and currency erosion.
  • Peak wedding (shaadi) seasons and post-Eid celebrations drive strong seasonal showroom demand.

Buying Investment Bars vs Bridal Jewellery: Practical Tips for Pakistani Buyers

Before purchasing gold in Pakistan, decide whether your goal is pure financial protection or ceremonial wear. If you are saving for the future, buying 24K (999) gold bars, ten-tola biscuits, or small ary-stamped wafers is far more efficient than buying jewellery. Pure bullion bars carry minimal making charges and zero ratti deductions when sold back in the wholesale sarafa market, allowing you to capture almost the entire movement of the live 24K gold rate.

When shopping for 22K or 21K wedding jewellery, never rely solely on verbal assurances of karat purity. Modern reputable jewellers in Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, and other major cities use electronic karat-testing machines (XRF analysers) that measure exact gold fineness without scratching or acid-testing the ornament. Asking for a computerized lab test slip alongside your showroom invoice provides ironclad proof of purity.

Always keep your original stamped purchase receipt in a safe place. In Pakistan's bazaar culture, an original receipt showing net weight, karat purity, and the shop's official stamp ensures smooth, dispute-free buyback or exchange whenever your family decides to upgrade or liquidate the piece.

  • Choose 24K (999) bars or biscuits for pure investment to avoid mazdoori and resale katoti deductions.
  • Request a computerized karatmeter (XRF) purity test slip when buying expensive 22K or 21K bridal sets.
  • Verify that electronic scales are zeroed in your view and note the weight in both grams and tolas.
  • Always preserve the stamped showroom receipt detailing net gold weight and guaranteed buyback terms.

What is the difference between 24K, 22K, and 21K gold in Pakistan?

Pure 24K gold (999 fineness) is soft unalloyed metal used for investment bars and biscuits. For jewellery, 22K (916 fineness) and 21K (875 fineness) are alloyed with small amounts of copper or silver to make durable bridal sets, bangles, and necklaces.

Why does the gold price in Pakistan change every day?

The local gold rate in Pakistan adjusts continuously based on two factors: the live international spot price of gold in US dollars and the daily exchange rate of the Pakistani rupee (PKR) against the US dollar, alongside local market supply and demand.

What are mazdoori and ratti deductions when buying or selling jewellery?

Mazdoori refers to the making charges or artisan labour fees added per gram or per tola when buying new jewellery. Ratti deduction (katoti) is a small weight or purity allowance that traditional jewellers sometimes deduct when buying back old ornaments to cover solder and refining loss.

Which city sets the benchmark gold rate in Pakistan?

Karachi serves as the primary bullion hub in Pakistan, and rates announced by the Karachi Sarafa market act as the national reference benchmark followed closely by Lahore, Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Peshawar, and Multan.

Is it better to buy 24K biscuits or 22K jewellery for investment in Pakistan?

For pure financial investment, 24K bars, wafers, and biscuits are superior because they carry negligible making charges and command full pure-metal value upon resale, whereas jewellery incurs mazdoori and potential resale deductions.

How can I verify that 22K gold jewellery in Pakistan is genuine?

Look for hallmark stamps inside the piece, buy from established jewellers who provide a written buyback guarantee on a stamped invoice, and ask for an electronic karat-testing machine (XRF) certificate confirming 916 or 875 fineness.

Understanding the Sovereign (Pawn / Pound) and Gram Weights in Sri Lanka

In Sri Lanka, the cornerstone unit of retail gold trade is the sovereign, which locals across Sinhala, Tamil, and English conversations commonly refer to as a pawn or a gold pound (pavan in Tamil). In modern Sri Lankan jewellery trade, one pawn or sovereign is standardised at exactly 8 grams of gold. Whenever families discuss the budget for a bridal necklace, a thali / kurukku, or a set of bangles, the quantity is almost always counted in pawns—such as a half-pawn ring (4 grams), a one-pawn chain (8 grams), or a five-pawn necklace.

While shoppers speak in pawns, every registered showroom in Sri Lanka weighs ornaments on calibrated electronic scales in metric grams and milligrams. Because one pawn equals 8 grams, converting between the two units is straightforward: simply multiply the 1 gram gold rate by eight to verify the 1 sovereign (8 grams) price shown in our live table above.

For larger bullion transactions or international comparisons, our price-by-weight table also lists standard metric increments including 10 grams, 20 grams, 1 troy ounce (31.1 grams), 50 grams, and 100 grams. Keeping the 8 grams pawn relationship clearly in mind ensures you never confuse a Sri Lankan 8-gram pawn quote with the 10-gram benchmark used in parts of India or the 11.66-gram tola used in Northern South Asia.

  • 1 Sovereign / Pawn / Pound: Standardized at exactly 8 grams in Sri Lankan jewellery trade.

  • 1 Gram: The metric unit used on computerized showroom scales for exact billing.

24K vs 22K Gold Purity in Sri Lanka: Which Should You Buy?

Sri Lankan gold buyers primarily choose between two purity grades: 24K and 22K, with 18K playing a supporting role for gemstone settings. At the top of the purity chart is 24K gold (stamped 999 fineness), which consists of pure unalloyed gold. Because 24 carat metal is naturally soft and malleable, it is unsuitable for intricate clasp work or daily-wear rings; instead, Sri Lankan buyers purchase 24K in the form of investment biscuits, small bullion bars, and pure coins.

For virtually all traditional and bridal jewellery in Sri Lanka, 22K gold—universally identified by its 916 hallmark stamp—is the undisputed island-wide standard. By alloying twenty-two parts pure gold with two parts copper, silver, or zinc, goldsmiths achieve a durable, resilient metal that holds intricate Kandyan bridal designs, traditional Tamil thali chains, and modern machine-cut bangles while preserving a rich, unmistakable golden glow. Consequently, the 22k gold price in Sri Lanka today is the benchmark most shoppers track daily.

When purchasing jewellery set with natural sapphires, rubies, diamonds, or semi-precious Ceylon stones, buyers also encounter 18K gold (stamped 750 fineness). Because 18 carat gold contains a higher proportion of strengthening alloy, its prongs grip precious gemstones much more securely than softer 22K metal.

  • 24K (999 fineness): Pure unalloyed gold reserved for investment biscuits, coins, and bullion wafers.
  • 22K (916 fineness): The premier Sri Lankan standard for bridal necklaces, thali chains, bangles, and rings.
  • 18K (750 fineness): Durable alloy ideal for jewellery mounted with Ceylon sapphires, diamonds, and gemstones.
  • Always inspect the inner band or clasp of any ornament for the 916 or 750 fineness hallmark.

Why Final Jewellery Prices Differ: Making Charges, Wastage, and Taxes

When you select a 22K necklace or pair of bangles in a Sri Lankan showroom, the final invoice is always higher than the simple multiplication of the item's weight by the live 22K per-gram or per-pawn rate. This difference comes from making charges (craftsmanship labour) and wastage allowances added by the jeweller to cover the cost of transforming raw bullion into finished ornaments.

Simple machine-manufactured chains, plain wedding bands, or stamped coins carry relatively low making charges per pawn. Conversely, intricate handcrafted Kandyan bridal sets (such as the traditional seven-necklace mala set), elaborate Tamil bridal harams, or antique filigree designs require extensive artisan labour and solder work, commanding higher craftsmanship fees. Additionally, national import duties, Value Added Tax (VAT), and social security levies applicable to commercial jewellery businesses influence retail showroom spreads.

If you are buying jewellery set with Ceylon sapphires, cubic zirconia, or pearls, always ask the salesperson to weigh or specify the net gold weight separately from the stone weight. Paying the 22K gold price on heavy synthetic stones is one of the most common costly mistakes shoppers can easily avoid.

  • Showroom invoices combine net gold value (weight times the 22K rate) with making charges and applicable taxes.
  • Handcrafted Kandyan and Tamil bridal ornaments carry higher artisan labour fees than basic machine chains.
  • Insist that gemstone, zircon, and pearl weights are deducted so you only pay the gold rate on net metal.
  • Compare total out-the-door prices across multiple shops for the exact same net gram weight and 916 purity.

What Moves the Gold Price in Sri Lanka: LKR Exchange Rate, Economy, and Festivals

Two primary economic forces drive the daily Sri Lanka gold rate today: the international US dollar spot price of bullion and the exchange rate of the Sri Lankan rupee (LKR) against the US dollar. Because Sri Lanka imports its bullion requirements and global gold trades in US currency, any depreciation or appreciation of the LKR immediately alters the local per-pawn price. During periods when the Sri Lankan rupee strengthens, local gold prices can ease even if global spot gold holds steady; conversely, rupee weakness rapidly pushes domestic pawn prices upward.

Gold also plays a unique role in household banking across Sri Lanka through the widespread practice of gold pawning (ranahana / gold-backed micro-loans) offered by commercial banks and licensed finance companies. Because families know that 22K hallmarked jewellery can be pledged at any bank branch for immediate working capital, rural agricultural needs, or emergency funds, physical 22K gold enjoys constant island-wide demand.

Cultural and religious seasons further shape showroom activity. The Sinhala and Tamil New Year (Aluth Avurudu / Puthandu) in April, the Thai Pongal harvest festival, Deepavali, Akshaya Tritiya, and auspicious wedding seasons (nekath / muhurtham dates) generate strong retail buying across every province.

  • LKR-to-USD currency exchange rate shifts directly and immediately impact local pawn and gram prices.
  • Global spot bullion movements driven by central banks and inflation set the international baseline.
  • Widespread bank gold-pawning facilities make 22K jewellery a highly liquid household financial asset in Sri Lanka.
  • Sinhala and Tamil New Year, Thai Pongal, Deepavali, and auspicious wedding nekath dates drive peak seasonal demand.

Smart Buying, Pawning, and Hallmarking Tips for Sri Lankan Shoppers

To get the best value when buying gold in Sri Lanka, always prioritize certified purity and transparent documentation. Look for the 916 hallmark laser-stamped on 22K jewellery and the National Gem and Jewellery Authority (NGJA) certified assay marks where available. Many reputable showrooms in Colombo and major towns can also verify karat purity in seconds using non-destructive electronic XRF karatmeters right at the counter.

If you are buying gold partly as a financial safety net that might be pledged for a bank pawning advance or exchanged in the future, stick to solid 22K (916) chains, bangles, or 24K coins without heavy stone settings or hollow wax-filled interiors. Commercial banks value pledged ornaments strictly on the net pure gold content after deducting stone weight and testing karatage, so solid 916 pieces always secure the highest valuation.

Finally, always collect a detailed official invoice listing the exact metric weight in grams (alongside the pawn equivalent), the guaranteed karat purity (22K / 916 or 24K / 999), the separate making charge, and the showroom's specific buyback and exchange terms.

  • Choose solid 22K (916) ornaments without heavy stones if you want maximum bank pawning or resale value.
  • Verify the 916 or 999 hallmark stamp and request an electronic XRF purity test printout for expensive purchases.
  • Check the digital scale in exact metric grams and divide by eight to confirm the equivalent weight in pawns.
  • Keep your official stamped showroom invoice to guarantee smooth buybacks or exchanges later.

Choosing Between 22K, 21K, and 18K Gold in Bangladeshi Showrooms

Unlike markets where unalloyed bars dominate retail counters, consumer gold buying in Bangladesh focuses heavily on crafted ornaments classified into 22K, 21K, and 18K grades. At the top of the retail showcase is 22K gold (stamped 916 fineness), which represents the premier choice for Bengali bridal sets, heavy sitahar necklaces, chur bangles, and heirloom gifts. Because 22 carat gold combines maximum purity with just enough alloy to hold intricate filigree and naksha patterns, the 22K gold price in Bangladesh today per vori is the single most watched figure nationwide.

Right alongside 22K sits 21K gold (875 fineness), a time-tested standard across Bangladesh for everyday earrings, chains, finger rings, and traditional family ornaments. With a slightly higher proportion of strengthening alloy than 22K, 21 carat gold resists bending and surface wear during daily household use while preserving the deep, radiant golden hue valued in South Asian traditions.

For modern lightweight designs, fashion accessories, and gemstone or diamond-studded pieces, Bangladeshi jewellers commonly craft items in 18K gold (750 fineness). In local showrooms, you may also hear references to traditional method gold (often called sanatani gold), which derives from melted and re-refined old ornaments without fixed modern hallmarking. For guaranteed resale value, buyers should always choose hallmarked 22K, 21K, or 18K pieces with certified fineness stamps.

  • 22K (916 fineness): The gold standard in Bangladesh for wedding jewellery, heavy necklaces, and high-purity ornaments.
  • 21K (875 fineness): Durable and popular purity for regular-wear chains, bangles, and classic designs.
  • 18K (750 fineness): Strong alloy suited for stone-mounted rings, modern pendants, and lightweight fashion jewellery.
  • Always verify laser-engraved 916, 875, or 750 hallmark stamps rather than buying uncertified traditional gold.

How the Vori (Bhori), Ana, Ratti, and Gram System Works in Bangladesh

Understanding gold weights in Bangladesh requires familiarity with both the traditional Bengali vori system and modern metric grams. In Bangladesh, the universal unit of gold measurement is the vori—frequently spelled bhori—which is identical in mass to one traditional South Asian tola, or 11.66 grams (specifically 11.6638 grams). Whether a family is budgeting for a wedding or checking a newspaper headline, the bangladesh gold rate today 22k vori is the primary reference number.

Inside traditional and modern jewellery shops alike, one vori is subdivided into smaller traditional fractions: sixteen anas make one vori, six rattis make one ana, and ten points make one ratti. When a goldsmith designs a pair of jhumka earrings or a delicate chain, the weight is often discussed in anas and rattis—for example, four anas (a quarter of a vori) or eight anas (half a vori).

To modernize trade and simplify calculations, BAJUS and urban jewellers now display prices per 1 gram alongside the per-vori tariff, and digital showroom scales weigh every ornament in exact metric grams before converting to vori, ana, and ratti on the receipt. Our price-by-weight table above shows both 1 gram, 10 grams, and 1 tola / vori (11.66 grams) so you can cross-check any jeweller's math effortlessly.

  • 1 Vori / Bhori: The classic Bangladeshi gold unit, equal to 1 tola or 11.66 grams.
  • 1 Ana: One-sixteenth of a vori, widely used by shoppers ordering lightweight rings, nose pins, and earrings.
  • 1 Ratti: Traditional fractional unit equal to one-sixth of an ana (ninety-six rattis per vori).
  • 1 Gram: The metric standard used on calibrated digital scales and modern BAJUS price circulars.

Role of BAJUS and Why Local Retail Rates Differ from Global Spot Prices

First-time observers often notice a difference between the international spot-derived gold price in Bangladesh and the retail showroom rates fixed by the Bangladesh Jewellers Association (BAJUS). While our live table reflects the direct conversion of global wholesale bullion into Bangladeshi taka, BAJUS sets domestic retail prices based on the local price of Refined Acid Gold (tezaab gold) in the domestic wholesale market, combined with import duties, banking costs, and supply conditions.

Because Bangladesh has historically had strict controls and formal duties on commercial bullion imports, combined with significant inflows of baggage-rule gold brought by expatriates returning from the Middle East, domestic wholesale supply can tighten or loosen independently of London or New York trading desks. When local refined gold becomes scarce or the taka weakens in the informal and banking channels, BAJUS revises the per-vori tariff upward.

Tracking both the live international spot benchmark in our table above and local BAJUS showroom circulars gives Bangladeshi buyers a complete view of the market. When global spot prices surge, local BAJUS rates almost invariably follow with an upward revision shortly afterward, making the live spot chart a valuable early indicator for wedding shoppers.

Making Charges (Mojuri), VAT, and Resale Deductions in Bangladesh

When you purchase an ornament from a jeweller in Dhaka, Chattogram, Khulna, or Rajshahi, the published price per vori covers only the raw gold alloy. To complete the purchase, the showroom adds two mandatory charges: artisan making charges—known in Bengali as mojuri—and statutory Value Added Tax (VAT) levied on retail jewellery sales.

Mojuri varies significantly depending on whether an ornament is machine-crafted or painstakingly handmade by skilled karigors (goldsmiths). Intricate bridal sitahar, mantasha, or filigree work requires days of manual craftsmanship and therefore commands higher mojuri per vori than simple chains or plain bands. In addition, Bangladeshi tax rules apply Value Added Tax on the retail invoice alongside minimum per-vori fabrication guidelines encouraged by trade associations.

Buyers should also understand the rules for exchanging or selling old gold back to a showroom. Under standard BAJUS practices, when you return hallmarked jewellery with its original cash memo to sell for cash or exchange for new ornaments, the jeweller deducts a fixed percentage from the current metal value—and neither the original mojuri nor the VAT paid at purchase is refunded. Knowing these rules upfront helps you negotiate fairly and preserve all original receipts.

  • Final showroom price equals the gold weight value (22K, 21K, or 18K) plus mojuri (making charge) and VAT.
  • Handcrafted bridal designs and antique naksha work carry higher mojuri than simple machine-made chains.
  • Ensure stone weight, enamel, and thread couplings are excluded from the net gold weight before billing.
  • Showrooms apply a standard percentage deduction when buying back or exchanging old gold ornaments.

What Moves the Gold Rate in Bangladesh: Taka Exchange Rate, Remittances, and Wedding Seasons

The gold rate in Bangladesh today responds to a combination of global financial forces and distinct regional dynamics. Internationally, movements in the US dollar spot price driven by central bank purchases, inflation data, and geopolitical uncertainty establish the baseline direction. Domestically, the exchange rate of the Bangladeshi taka (BDT) against the US dollar acts as a powerful multiplier: whenever the taka depreciates, importing or sourcing bullion becomes more expensive in local currency, pushing per-vori prices to new highs.

Expatriate remittances and passenger baggage allowances also play a major role in Bangladesh's gold economy. Millions of Bangladeshi workers residing in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain, and Malaysia regularly bring back 22K ornaments or gold bars when visiting home, linking the Dhaka bullion market closely with Gulf retail centres.

On the demand side, the winter wedding season—running from late autumn through spring—along with major religious festivals like Eid-ul-Fitr, Eid-ul-Adha, and Durga Puja, creates intense seasonal buying across the country. During these peak months, jewellery enclaves such as Baitul Mukarram and Gulshan pink city shopping complexes see heavy foot traffic and firm making charges.

  • BDT-to-USD exchange rate movements directly influence the domestic cost of bullion in Bangladesh.
  • Global spot price trends set the underlying momentum for local BAJUS price revisions.
  • Expatriate remittances and Gulf passenger inflows strongly shape physical supply and household demand.
  • Winter wedding seasons, Eid celebrations, and Puja festivals drive peak retail jewellery purchases.

Practical Tips Before Buying Gold in Bangladesh

Whether you are purchasing a single 22K ring or a complete bridal set, taking a few practical precautions protects your hard-earned money. First, always verify that the ornament carries a clear laser hallmark indicating its exact purity—916 for 22K, 875 for 21K, or 750 for 18K—alongside the jeweller's registered identification mark. Many leading showrooms in Dhaka and Chattogram now validate purity using computerized X-ray fluorescence (XRF) karatmeters in front of the customer.

Second, pay close attention to how weight is recorded on your cash memo. Because scales measure in metric grams while prices are often discussed in vori, ana, and ratti, verify the exact gram weight on the digital display and use our calculator above to confirm the conversion. Make sure any decorative stones, pearls, or non-gold clasps are weighed separately and subtracted from the net gold weight.

Finally, never leave the showroom without an official stamped cash memo and VAT invoice. This document must state the exact karat purity, net weight in both grams and vori/ana/ratti, making charges, VAT, and the store's specific exchange and cash-buyback deduction terms.

  • Insist on laser-hallmarked jewellery stamped 916 (22K), 875 (21K), or 750 (18K) and request an XRF test printout.
  • Check the digital scale in grams first, then verify the conversion into vori, ana, and ratti.
  • Confirm that artificial stones, pearls, and lac fillings are deducted from the net gold weight.
  • Keep your stamped showroom cash memo safe to secure the best rate during future exchanges or buybacks.

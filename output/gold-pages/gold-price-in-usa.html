Which Gold Purity to Buy in the USA: 24K, 22K, 18K, 14K, and 10K Explained

Selecting the right purity depends entirely on whether you are buying precious metals for investment storage or personal adornment. In the American bullion sector, 24K gold (marked 999 or 9999 fine) and 22K gold (marked 916 fine) dominate investor portfolios. Pure 24K metal is the standard for cast and minted bullion bars as well as the American Gold Buffalo coin, while 22K is famously used for the official American Gold Eagle bullion coin, which alloys pure gold with a small balance of silver and copper to improve scratch resistance during handling.

In American retail jewelry stores, 14K gold (585 fineness) is by far the most popular choice for engagement rings, wedding bands, and everyday chains. Because pure gold is naturally soft, alloying fourteen parts pure gold with ten parts durable metals creates a resilient piece that withstands daily wear while maintaining an attractive warm luster and an accessible price point. High-end designer boutiques and luxury watchmakers in the United States frequently step up to 18K gold (750 fineness), which delivers a noticeably richer golden saturation while remaining sturdy enough for fine gemstone settings.

Under United States federal stamping standards, 10K gold is the minimum karat purity that can legally be marketed and sold as solid gold jewelry. While 10K pieces offer maximum hardness and budget-friendly pricing, they contain less than half pure gold by weight. Meanwhile, the 22K gold price in USA today is closely followed by South Asian, Middle Eastern, and Mediterranean American communities who purchase traditional high-purity bridal and heirloom ornaments.

  • 24K (9999 fine): Pure investment-grade metal used in American Gold Buffalo coins and certified refinery bullion bars.
  • 22K (916 fine): Traditional high-karat jewelry alloy and the official composition of the American Gold Eagle coin.
  • 18K (750 fine): Luxury jewelry standard balancing rich color saturation with reliable structural strength.
  • 14K (585 fine) and 10K: The workhorse alloys of American mainstream retail jewelry, prized for scratch resistance.

Gold Weights Used in the United States: Troy Ounces, Grams, Kilos, and Tolas

Understanding weight conversions is one of the most important skills for any American bullion buyer. Precious metals in the United States are weighed exclusively in troy ounces rather than the standard avoirdupois ounces used on kitchen and postal scales. One troy ounce equals approximately 31.1 grams, making it noticeably heavier than a regular household ounce. Whenever you see the gold price today USA per ounce, that figure strictly applies to a full troy ounce of fine metal.

For smaller investments and jewelry appraisals, metric grams have become universal across US bullion dealers and pawn shops. Popular retail bar sizes include 1 gram, 5 grams, 10 grams, 20 grams, 50 grams, and 100 grams, alongside fractional coin denominations of one-tenth, one-quarter, and one-half troy ounce. At the institutional and high-net-worth level, 1000 grams (the 1 kilogram bar) is widely traded for vault storage due to its lower fabrication cost per gram.

Many families and diaspora buyers across major American metropolitan areas also track the gold price in USA per tola and per 10 grams when shopping at specialty ethnic jewelers. American Bullion Coins vs. Cast and Minted Gold Bars

Investors entering the US physical market typically choose between sovereign-minted coins and privately refined bullion bars. The flagship coins produced by the United States Mint—the 22K American Gold Eagle and the 24K American Gold Buffalo—are backed by the US government for weight and purity. Each 1 oz coin contains one full troy ounce of pure gold; the American Gold Eagle weighs slightly more in gross terms only because its copper-silver alloy is added on top of the full troy ounce of pure gold.

Because sovereign coins carry legal tender status, intricate anti-counterfeiting features, and instant recognition at every coin shop in North America, they command a slightly higher dealer premium over the live spot price. However, that premium is often partially recovered when you sell, as dealers frequently offer tighter buy-back spreads on recognizable US Mint coins compared to generic bullion products.

Certified gold bars from LBMA-accredited refineries offer a straightforward way to accumulate more pure metal per dollar invested. Available in minted assay-carded formats from 1 gram up to 100 grams, as well as larger cast loaves, bars generally carry lower manufacturing markups than government coins. Keep bars sealed inside their original tamper-evident assay packaging with matching serial numbers to preserve full resale value.

  • American Gold Eagle (22K) and Gold Buffalo (24K) coins each contain a full troy ounce of fine gold in the 1 oz size.
  • Sovereign coins offer unmatched liquidity and easy authentication at local coin shops across all states.
  • Minted and cast bars from accredited refiners deliver lower premiums per gram, especially in 1 oz, 100 gram, and kilo sizes.
  • Always keep assay cards intact and unblemished when storing modern minted gold bars.

Why Local US Dealer and Jewelry Shop Prices Differ from the Spot Rate

A common question among first-time buyers checking the usa gold rate today is why no dealer sells physical coins or jewelry at the exact spot number shown on financial tickers. The spot price reflects wholesale settlement for large institutional bars before consumer fabrication. Transforming raw refined grain into a mirror-finish coin, a serialized assay bar, or an intricate necklace requires minting machinery, skilled labor, security transport, and quality assurance.

In the bullion market, this difference is called the dealer premium over spot. Fractional gold coins and 1 gram bars carry higher percentage premiums because stamping and packaging a tiny piece costs nearly as much as producing a full 1 oz bar. Furthermore, during periods of heightened retail demand, wholesale mints may allocate inventory, causing physical dealer premiums to widen even if the paper spot price remains calm.

In retail jewelry stores, markups above intrinsic melt value are substantially wider. A 14K or 18K piece sold at a mall retailer or boutique incorporates craftsmanship fees, stone setting labor, brand marketing, showroom rent, and retail profit margins. In addition, state and local sales tax rules vary across the United States; while many states exempt qualifying investment bullion purchases above certain thresholds, standard retail jewelry is almost universally subject to local sales tax.

  • Bullion premiums cover refining, minting dies, tamper-evident assay packaging, and armored logistics.
  • Smaller fractional units (such as 1 gram bars or tenth-ounce coins) carry higher per-gram premiums than 1 oz products.
  • Retail jewelry prices include substantial design, labor, and storefront markups far above raw karat melt value.
  • State sales tax exemptions often apply to qualifying bullion coins and bars, though rules vary by state jurisdiction.

COMEX Futures, the Federal Reserve, and What Moves US Gold Prices

The United States plays a central role in global precious metals price discovery, primarily through COMEX futures trading in New York alongside London over-the-counter wholesale clearing. Throughout the American trading session, institutional hedgers, mining producers, commercial banks, and asset managers trade standardized futures contracts, causing the live gold price in USA to tick up or down second by second in response to macroeconomic news.

Monetary policy decisions by the Federal Reserve are among the most powerful domestic drivers of the gold market. When real interest rates on US Treasury securities decline, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding physical bullion drops, typically boosting investor demand for gold. Conversely, aggressive rate tightening cycles or surging real bond yields can create headwinds for precious metals.

Inflation expectations, domestic banking stability, federal fiscal deficits, and the relative strength of the US dollar index also shape daily price action. When purchasing power concerns rise or equity markets experience sharp turbulence, American households and institutional allocators alike turn to physical bullion and precious metal funds as a time-tested portfolio diversifier.

  • COMEX trading in New York provides continuous liquidity and price discovery during North American market hours.
  • Federal Reserve interest rate guidance and US Treasury real yields heavily influence institutional allocation into bullion.
  • Consumer inflation data and employment reports frequently trigger immediate volatility in the live USD spot rate.
  • Global geopolitical uncertainty and central bank reserve buying reinforce long-term demand for US dollar gold.

Practical Tips for Buying, Verifying, and Selling Gold in the United States

Whether you are buying bullion online, visiting a local coin shop, or selling vintage jewelry, preparation protects your capital. Under the US National Gold and Silver Stamping Act, any quality mark stamped on an item (such as 14K, 18K, 22K, or 999) must be accompanied by a registered manufacturer trademark or hallmark identifying who stands behind the purity guarantee. Be cautious of unmarked pieces or vague stamps when evaluating estate jewelry.

Reputable US bullion dealers verify metal authenticity using non-destructive tools such as ultrasonic thickness gauges, electromagnetic conductivity testers, X-ray fluorescence analyzers, and precision troy scales. When buying online, choose established dealers who provide fully insured shipping, transparent live pricing locked at checkout, and clear buy-back policies.

If you are liquidating scrap 14K or 18K jewelry, weigh your items at home in grams first and use our calculator to find their exact fine gold melt value. Obtain quotes from multiple local coin dealers or refiners rather than settling for the first cash-for-gold storefront, as specialized bullion dealers typically pay a much higher share of the intrinsic spot price.

  • Look for both a karat purity stamp and a registered manufacturer trademark on all US jewelry pieces.
  • Compare total delivered cost—including dealer premium, shipping, insurance, and payment processing fees—before buying bullion.
  • Calculate your exact karat melt value per gram using the live table above before selling scrap jewelry to a dealer.
  • Store physical bullion in a UL-rated home safe, bank safe deposit box, or insured third-party depository.

Why does a 1 oz American Gold Eagle weigh more than one troy ounce?

An American Gold Eagle is minted in durable 22K gold (916 fineness), but by law it still contains one full troy ounce of pure 24K gold. The extra copper and silver alloy is added on top of that pure gold content, giving the coin a higher gross weight while its intrinsic gold value remains a full troy ounce.

What is the difference between 24K, 22K, 18K, and 14K gold in the US market?

24K is pure 9999 fine gold used for bullion bars and Buffalo coins, while 22K (916 fine) is used for Gold Eagle coins and high-purity cultural jewelry. 18K (750 fine) and 14K (585 fine) contain higher proportions of strengthening alloy metals, making them the standard choices for durable American rings, bracelets, and everyday fine jewelry.

Why is the retail price at a US coin shop higher than the live spot price?

The spot price reflects raw wholesale bullion traded in institutional volume. Retail coins and bars include a dealer premium that covers refining, minting dies, assay packaging, armored transport, insurance, and the local dealer's operating margin.

Is physical gold bullion subject to sales tax in the United States?

Sales tax treatment in the United States is governed at the state level rather than the federal level. Many states exempt investment-grade gold bullion coins and bars from state sales tax—sometimes subject to purity rules or minimum purchase amounts—while finished retail jewelry is generally taxable.
